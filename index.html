🌍 Community translation
Portuguese is Mozambique's sole official language (Constitution, Article 10). Emakhuwa, Xichangana, Cinyanja, Elomwe, Cisena, Echuwabo, Cindau and Xitswa are recognised national languages (Constitution, Article 9) that the state promotes as part of Mozambique's cultural and educational heritage — they are not official languages, and this tracker doesn't blur that distinction. Language figures are drawn from the 2017 census via Wikipedia's "Languages of Mozambique" article; the underlying primary source (Mozambique's INE 2017 census) could not be independently verified in this research pass. Portuguese ships with a best-effort draft translation of every UI string — none reviewed by a native speaker yet, treat it as a starting point. Cinyanja also ships a draft, reused from the Zimbabwe site's Chewa content since Chewa and Cinyanja are the same standard language (ISO 639-3 nya). Xichangana ships a draft too, reused from the Zimbabwe site's Shangani content, but at lower confidence since Xichangana and Shangani are closely related Tswa-Ronga cluster languages rather than a confirmed identical standard — please prioritize reviewing that one. Emakhuwa, Elomwe, Cisena, Echuwabo, Cindau and Xitswa are intentionally still blank rather than guessed: Cindau was meant to reuse the Zimbabwe site's Ndau content (the Ndau people span the Zimbabwe–Mozambique border), but that content is itself still blank on the Zimbabwe site as of this build, so there was nothing yet to reuse; the other four simply have no reliable draft source yet. Note that Xitswa (Tswa) is a distinct language from Xichangana/Tsonga despite the similar-sounding name, so it is deliberately not filled in from that content. Suggest corrections below for any of the nine languages shown; once a suggestion gets 2 community endorsements it's promoted to "verified" and goes live automatically.
